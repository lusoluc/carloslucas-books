Scrum Year in Review 2026: Fading in Big Tech, Growing Outside IT

Twenty-five years after the Agile Manifesto was drafted at Snowbird, 2026 brought a striking split. Inside software engineering, ceremony-heavy Scrum is quietly being retired or stripped down. Outside IT, in procurement, operations, HR, finance, and public services across four continents, practical Scrum habits are gaining real traction.

By Carlos Lucas · Reading time: 10 minutes · Published 27 November 2026

1. The 2026 Paradox: Less Visible in Software, More Needed Elsewhere

If you spent 2026 reading software engineering feeds, you could easily conclude that Scrum is over. In North American tech firms, standalone "ceremony-master" roles were cut or folded back into engineering and product leadership, and AI coding assistants changed how developers ship code day to day.

Yet step outside software departments and look at operations, procurement, supply chain, HR, or public administration, and the exact opposite picture emerges. While mature tech teams outgrew rigid Sprint rituals, non-IT departments discovered that their real bottleneck was never writing code: it was invisible backlogs, departmental silos, and polite status meetings that hid broken plans.

The numbers from 2025 and 2026 back up this split rather than a simple rise or fall:

  • Pure textbook Scrum is now the minority: According to the 18th State of Agile Report (Digital.ai), 74% of agile teams now run hybrid or self-developed models (48% hybrid, 26% custom), mixing Scrum goals with Kanban flow and continuous delivery.
  • Satisfaction fell where rituals replaced outcomes: Overall agile satisfaction dropped from 72% to 59%, while 76% of respondents reported stronger pressure to prove measurable business value, and 79% were asked to deliver more with fewer resources (Echometer Scrum Statistics 2026; Breeze.pm).
  • Business adoption is still shallow: Only 13% of organisations say agile ways of working are deeply embedded across the business, and 44% still track delivery on spreadsheets and static status reports (Breeze.pm).

2. The AI Reality Check: Faster Steps Do Not Fix Broken Queues

Every 2026 conference agenda was dominated by AI. Yet the empirical data released this autumn tells a far more sobering story than vendor marketing:

  • Employees save time, organisations lose it: In October 2026, Gartner reported that while 62% of employees said AI tools saved them time, only 7% of organisations gave staff guidance on how to reinvest those hours, and just 3% exceeded cost-saving targets (Scrum.org analysis of Gartner, Oct 2026).
  • Practitioners do not fear replacement; they fear shallow work: Scrum.org's AI4Agile Practitioners Report 2026 (289 practitioners surveyed) found that 83% use AI, mostly to cut documentation overhead (73.7% higher productivity, 71.6% lower cognitive load). Only 7.6% saw AI as a major job threat, while 54.3% named *integration uncertainty* as the top barrier: people have tools, not a clear workflow.
  • AI acts as a mirror and multiplier: As Google's DORA Report 2025 noted across nearly 5,000 technology professionals (90% AI usage, 30% low trust in AI outputs), AI speeds up cohesive teams and exposes broken handoffs in fragmented ones.

For non-IT teams, the takeaway is crystal clear: if an AI assistant helps someone draft a report or answer a customer ticket in five minutes instead of thirty, but that item still sits for six days waiting for an approval signature, the customer notices zero difference. The bottleneck sits in the operating model, not at the keyboard.

3. Where Scrum Grew in 2026: Procurement, Supply Chain, HR, and Public Services

One of the clearest 2026 signals came from the 2026 Global State of Agility in Procurement and Supply Study (Scrum.org and the Lean-Agile Procurement Alliance). Its conclusion speaks directly to operational leaders: AI and digital tools stall whenever procurement, finance, legal, and supply chain remain locked in sequential silos.

In parallel, EY's Agile Sourcing analysis documented how public-sector and life-sciences procurement teams are replacing rigid multi-month tenders with short iterative workshops and Scrum-inspired decision cycles. Similarly, Scrum Alliance's Annual Report highlighted growing demand in *Agile for HR*, *Agile for Marketing*, *Agile in Sales*, and *AI in Healthcare*, shifting the focus from "framework purists" to broad *organisational enablers*.

4. Four Continents, Four Distinct 2026 Realities

Scrum did not move in lockstep around the world in 2026. Comparing North America, Europe, South America, and Asia reveals why local context matters so much:

(US & Canada)		 "Post-Scrum" software teams using AI coding agents; steep consolidation of standalone Scrum Master jobs in Big Tech. (India, Japan, SG, CN)		 India centres on AI-assisted delivery; Japan blends Kaizen with Scrum (RSG Tokyo 2026); China ties Scrum to hardware & smart vehicles. RSG Singapore & RSG Taipei 2026 put agility into executive decision rooms; automotive and finance apply Scrum to physical products. In large outsourcing hubs, practitioners report a gap between the "Agile" label and genuine team decision-making autonomy.

5. What This Means for Non-IT Teams Entering 2027

The biggest lesson of 2026 is liberating: you do not need to copy Silicon Valley, and you do not need a 200-page scaling manual. Tech companies are discarding Scrum *rituals* because their deployment pipelines already run in minutes. Operational teams outside IT still face weeks-long approval queues, invisible ad-hoc favours, and single-person legacy bottlenecks.

For your team in 2027, keep the mechanics that actually remove pain (one visible board, honest capacity limits, short review cycles, and clear ownership) and drop everything that feels like theatre.

Quick Practitioner Answers

Is Scrum dying in IT in 2026?

Ceremony-heavy textbook Scrum and standalone Scrum Master titles declined sharply in North American software firms as AI coding tools and continuous delivery took over. However, 74% of agile teams still use hybrid or tailored models based on Scrum's core rhythms.

Why is Scrum growing outside IT while shrinking inside tech?

Software teams already had fast feedback loops. Non-IT departments such as procurement, HR, finance, supply chain, and public services still suffer from hidden backlogs, departmental silos, and endless status meetings, so lightweight Scrum solves an immediate daily pain there.

Did AI replace Scrum Teams or Product Owners in 2026?

No. Gartner reported in October 2026 that 62% of employees saved time with AI, yet only 7% of organisations guided how to reinvest that time and only 3% exceeded cost-saving targets. Prioritisation and cross-team alignment remain human decisions.

2027 Board Reset

Five Habits to Keep in 2027 (and Three to Drop)

  1. Keep: One shared visual board where planned projects and daily fires sit side by side.
  2. Keep: Honest capacity planning that reserves space for real interruptions instead of pretending 100% is available.
  3. Keep: Short, plain-English Sprint Reviews showing finished work rather than green status slides.
  4. Keep: Clear Product Owner trade-offs whenever AI or automation frees up team hours.
  5. Keep: Simple WIP limits (max two active items per person) so work actually finishes.
  6. Drop: Story-point theatre and velocity targets that measure busyness instead of customer impact.
  7. Drop: Heavy scaling ceremonies copied blindly from software handbooks into 20-person business units.
  8. Drop: "Agile in name only" rollouts where leadership keeps every old status meeting intact.
